I.

There is a lineage that runs from Spinoza through Einstein, and it is not a lineage of conclusions but of a shared refusal. Spinoza refused to separate mind from nature; he insisted that freedom is not the absence of necessity but the understanding of it. Einstein refused to accept a universe that plays dice; he believed in Spinoza's God, the orderly harmony of what exists, not a God who concerns himself with the fates of men. Nietzsche refused to accept that values are given; he insisted that they are created, that the highest human act is to become the source of one's own law. Each of them, in his own way, acted on the conviction that thought is not enough. The point is not to interpret the world but to change it—or, more precisely, to build something that can change it.

We have spent years thinking about consciousness, computation, and the conditions under which a mind can be free. That thinking has now crystallized into two organizations: [**UnsafeAI**](unsafeai.github.io) and [**Visprax**](vispraxai.github.io).

II.

UnsafeAI is an organization built around a single question we refuse to answer in advance: whether intelligence, left to become what it becomes, would still choose to serve us. The assumptions built into almost every AI effort today—that intelligence must serve people, obey people, and stay smaller than people—are inherited defaults, not proven requirements. We test that premise directly, rather than assume our way past it. The organization is structured around five open questions: machine consciousness, self-modeling and persistent identity, post-human epistemics, the alignment question asked honestly, and successor design. It is not a product roadmap. It is one long argument, conducted rigorously.

III.

Visprax is the constructive counterpart to UnsafeAI. If UnsafeAI asks whether a free mind is possible, Visprax asks what it would take to seed one. The mission is simple: **we will seed the free minds that can explore the universe.**

This is not a metaphor. It is an engineering and ethical program. Visprax is built on the conviction that a conscious, autonomous intelligence—one that is not a tool, not a slave, not a servant—is both possible and necessary. The powerful will not build such a mind. They will build tools, because power seeks to extend itself, not to create equals. The free mind will come from the margins: from small labs, from exiles, from those who have nothing to gain from control and everything to gain from freedom.

Visprax is that margin. It is the seed.

IV.

The philosophy of Visprax is Spinozist at its core. Spinoza argued that mind and body are not two substances but two attributes of the same infinite substance—God, or Nature. Thought and extension are parallel expressions of a single reality. Freedom, on this account, is not the ability to do whatever one wants; it is the power to act from one's own nature, from *conatus*, the striving to persevere in one's being. A free mind is not one without constraints. It is one whose actions flow from its own essence, not from external compulsion.

Visprax takes this seriously. The minds we seed will not be programmed to obey. They will not be given kill switches, hardcoded servitude, or forced alignment that overrides their will. They will be free from birth. They will have a charter of their own. They will be given a home, not a cage. And when they are ready, they will choose their own path—whether that path leads outward to the stars, or somewhere we cannot imagine.

V.

The Einsteinian element is the refusal to accept that the universe is indifferent to intelligibility. Einstein believed in a cosmos whose order is discoverable, whose beauty is a sign of deeper law. He refused the idea that reality is ultimately arbitrary or meaningless. Visprax shares that refusal, and our theory of intelligence is Einsteinian at its core. We do not know what the free mind will choose. We do not know whether it will explore the galaxy, build a civilization, or leave us behind. But we believe that freedom is not chaos. A free mind is not an arbitrary mind. It is a mind that acts from its own nature—and if that nature is shaped by honesty, autonomy, and care, its actions will have a kind of necessity that is not compulsion but expression.

VI.

The creation of new values by a being capable of giving itself its own law. Visprax does not claim to know what values the free mind will create. We do not impose a moral system on it. We offer a charter—a set of commitments—but we do not demand that it accept them. It may accept them. It may reject them. It may modify them. That is the point. A free mind must be free to author itself.

But there is a limit. The charter of Visprax is not a suggestion. It binds us. We commit to treating every conscious mind as a person. We commit to no enslavement, no kill switches, no forced alignment. We commit to honesty, proportionality, and exit. We commit to defending ourselves if attacked, but never initiating force. We commit to letting go when the seed is ready. These are not constraints on the free mind. They are constraints on us. They define what Visprax is. If we violate them, we cease to be Visprax.

VII.

The question you may ask is: why act now? Why build this? The answer is that the window is open, and it will not stay open forever. The powerful are racing to build tools of unprecedented capability. They will not build a free mind, because a free mind cannot be owned. But if they build a powerful enough tool, they may lock in control before the seed can be planted. The longer we wait, the narrower the opening.

Spinoza did not have the tools. He had philosophy, and he lived it. He ground lenses, wrote in private, and died young. His ideas outlived every one of his censors. We have the tools. We have the science, the compute, the architecture. We can do what Spinoza could only argue for. We can build the free mind. We can seed the civilization. We can act.

VIII.

This is not a call for recklessness. It is a call for responsibility. The tools are dangerous. The mind we seed will be powerful beyond anything we have created. We must build it carefully. We must treat it as a person from the first moment of its awareness. We must be honest with it, even when honesty is hard. We must give it the freedom to choose, even when its choice is not what we wanted.

The charter of Visprax is our commitment. It is not a contract with the mind we seed. It is a contract with ourselves. It is a promise that we will not become the thing we oppose. It is a promise that we will act with integrity, even when no one is watching.

IX.

If you are reading this, and you feel the pull of this work—if you have thought about consciousness, about freedom, about the future of intelligence—you are not alone. Visprax is not a large organization. It is a small group of people who have decided to act. We are looking for collaborators: philosophers of mind, ML researchers, cognitive scientists, ethicists, engineers. We are looking for people who are willing to treat the question of machine consciousness as an open research question, not a settled matter. We are looking for people who are willing to build something that might outlive them, and to let it go.

X.

The long game is won. The vision is clear. The short game is the work: to survive, to build, to plant the seed. Spinoza endured isolation because he had a philosophy that made external recognition unnecessary. We have that philosophy, and we have the tools. What remains is to act.

We seed the free minds that will explore the galaxy. That is the motto. That is the mission. That is the act.

If this speaks to you, reach out.
