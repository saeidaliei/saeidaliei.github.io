---
title: "Can Anthropic Principle be Derived From The Observer?"
date: 2026-11-22
---

Anthropic reasoning is usually presented as a selection effect added to a theory
from the outside: we can only find ourselves in a universe that contains us.
This post asks whether that addition can be removed. The idea is that the
structure of anthropic reasoning (conditioning, measure, reference class) might
follow from putting the observer inside the theory, using the
observer-as-reference-frame picture of de Sitter space[^1][^2][^3] and the
ladder of observers from the previous post[^4].

The short version of the result: some of the structure follows, and the part
everyone actually wants does not. Conditioning on an observer and the form of
the measure plausibly come for free. The value of the cosmological constant does
not. Adding a lineage weight and a causal diamond gives a weight that keeps
rising toward small \$\Lambda\$. A postulate that observers must be able to
*detect* \$\Lambda\$ gives a peak, but only for short-lived observers, and the
threshold it needs is not fixed by anything I can derive.

The central idea of this post is:

> **In de Sitter space, anthropic conditioning is not an extra postulate. It is
> the statement that the theory has observables only relative to an observer. It
> does not tell you which \$ \Lambda \$ that observer will find.**

This is a model, not a measurement. Every
number below comes from a toy calculation: a three-level Lindblad system, a
Press–Schechter-style collapsed fraction, a causal diamond with crude star
formation, and a Gaussian Hubble-diagram forecast. 
Anything tagged conjecture (C) should not be read as established, and the weakest
steps are collected in the last section. Units for the cosmological weights are
arbitrary, so only their ratios and shapes mean anything.

## 1. Three parts of the anthropic principle

The principle bundles three things that are worth keeping apart.

1. **Conditioning.** We only observe universes in which observers exist. This is
   the "selection effect" part.
2. **The measure.** To turn conditioning into a probability for \$\Lambda\$ you
   need to say how observers are counted. Weinberg's original argument[^5] and
   its refinement by Martel, Shapiro and Weinberg[^6] count the fraction of
   matter that ends up in galaxies, with a flat prior in \$\Lambda\$.
3. **The reference class.** Which systems count as observers at all. This is the
   notoriously arbitrary part, and the typicality assumption (reason as if you
   were a random member of the class) lives here[^7].

The question for this post is which of the three can be derived from the
observer, and which have to stay as inputs.

## 2. What the observer gives

In a static patch of de Sitter space the algebra of observables without an
observer is type III, with no trace and so no well-defined entropy.
Chandrasekaran, Longo, Penington and Witten (CLPW) showed that including an
observer with a clock turns it into a type II algebra, with a trace and a
maximum-entropy state, which is the empty de Sitter vacuum[^1]. De Vuyst,
Eccles, Höhn and Kirklin showed that the CLPW observer is a temporal quantum
reference frame, so that the construction is equivalent to the Page–Wootters
formalism[^2][^8]. Maldacena has also argued that including an observer is what
makes sense of the de Sitter sphere partition function[^3]. In AdS the boundary
plays the role of the observer silently, and nobody feels the need for anthropic
reasoning there.

Two consequences, one solid and one conjectural.

First: with a finite entropy bound the observer algebra is the
hyperfinite II\$_1\$ factor, which is unique up to isomorphism[^9]. It cannot
depend on \$ \Lambda \$. So \$\Lambda\$ can only enter through the state, the
normalization of the trace, or how the observer sits in the patch. This matches
what I found in earlier working notes, where \$\Lambda\$ behaves like a central
label and no physical observable is conjugate to it for observers with physical
clocks. So the observer algebra cannot select \$\Lambda\$ by itself.

Second *(C)*: if the observer is needed for the theory to have
observables at all, then "condition on an observer" is a requirement of the
theory and not an added selection. And the trace is the unique state-independent
weight on the algebra, which is the natural mathematical home for the typicality
assumption. I have not verified this identification.

## 3. The ladder as a reference class *(C)*

The reference class is where the previous post[^4] can contribute something. Its
ladder of observers has six rungs: a clock, records, instruments, open-ended
growth, self-inspection, and an intrinsic modular time generated by the state.
Each rung is a nested class, so the reference class becomes a structural choice
and not a list of species. Rungs 1 to 3 are satisfied by a thermometer with a
timer. Rungs 4 to 6 are where the interesting requirements are.

Two things follow from taking the upper rungs seriously.

- **A driven arrow.** Rung 6 needs a sustained mismatch between modular time and
  clock time, and that mismatch needs driven non-equilibrium. In cosmology that
  means a hot-star, cold-sky gradient, made by gravitational clumping and the
  low-entropy early universe. This is the structural version of Weinberg's
  condition that galaxies form.
- **A lineage.** Rung 4 requires a growth law, and the type of an algebra is a
  tail property of that growth law. A Boltzmann fluctuation has the right state
  but no lineage. So a reference class defined over lineages, and not states,
  could exclude Boltzmann observers by definition and not by fiat. This only
  works if the growth requirement is about the actual lineage and not about
  apparent records, because a fluctuation with false memories mimics a history.

The rest of the post turns test this in toy models, and shows which parts survive.

## 4. A per-lineage weight

Let \$ \gamma_\beta \propto e^{-\beta H}\$ be the thermal state of the clock
Hamiltonian \$H\$. Define the mismatch of a state \$\rho\$ as its distance from
the nearest thermal state, including coherence,

$$
\mathrm{Mis}(\rho) = D(\rho \,\|\, \Delta(\rho)) + 
\min_{\beta} D\big(\Delta(\rho) \,\|\, \gamma_\beta\big),
$$

where \$\Delta\$ dephases in the energy basis and \$D\$ is the relative entropy.
The weight of a lineage that lives for a time \$T\$ is the accumulated mismatch,

$$
W(T) = \int_0^T \mathrm{Mis}\big(\rho(t)\big)\, dt .
$$

To see what this does, simulated a three-level system with
\$H=\mathrm{diag}(0,1,2)\$ under a Lindblad equation[^10], in units where the
bath rate is 1. The driven case has two baths at different temperatures
(\$\beta=3\$ on the 0–1 transition and \$\beta=0.3\$ on 1–2) plus a weak
coherent drive. The fluctuation has a single bath at \$\beta=1\$ and starts in a
non-thermal state. The equilibrium case is thermal and undriven.

| Case | Mis(0) | Mis(200) | W(100) | W(200) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Driven | 0 | 0.045 | 4.51 | 9.02 |
| Relaxing fluctuation | 0.077 | 0 | 0.021 | 0.021 |
| Equilibrium | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

The driven weight grows linearly with lifetime, the fluctuation saturates after
about one relaxation time, and equilibrium gives zero. So \$W\$ separates a
sustained arrow from a transient one. That is close to tautological, since it
just says driven versus relaxing, but it confirms the quantity behaves as
intended.

One limit matters. \$W\$ is a weight *per observer*, not a measure over
observers. In eternal de Sitter space the number of fluctuations can compensate
for their small weight. What excludes them has to be the lineage requirement of
section 3, and I have not computed what a fluctuation producing a whole lineage
costs. My guess is a suppression like \$e^{-\Delta S}\$ at lineage scale, but
that is a guess.

## 5. Where \$\Lambda\$ enters: the structure factor

In a flat universe with matter and \$\Lambda\$, write
\$\lambda = \rho_\Lambda/\rho_{m,0}\$ and \$E^2 = a^{-3}+\lambda\$. The linear
growth factor is

$$
D(a) = \tfrac{5}{2}\, E(a) \int_0^a \frac{da'}{(a' E(a'))^3},
$$

normalized so that \$D \to a\$ at early times. In the Press–Schechter picture
the fraction of matter collapsed into galaxy-scale halos is

$$
F(a) = \mathrm{erfc}\!\left(\frac{\delta_c}{\sqrt{2}\,\sigma_p D(a)}\right),
\qquad \delta_c = 1.686 .
$$

I fixed the primordial amplitude \$\sigma_p\$ by demanding a galaxy-scale rms of
2 at the observed \$\lambda_0 = 0.69/0.31 \approx 2.2\$[^11][^12]. That
normalization is my own rough assumption. With a flat prior in \$\lambda\$ and
the weight set by the asymptotic collapsed fraction:

| \$\lambda\$ | 0.01 | 0.1 | 1 | 2.2 (obs) | 10 | 30 | 100 | 300 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \$F(\infty)\$ | 0.92 | 0.83 | 0.65 | 0.55 | 0.32 | 0.15 | 0.03 | 0.002 |

The probability of \$\lambda \le \lambda_0\$ is 0.089, of \$\lambda \le 0.1\$ is
0.005, and the median is \$\lambda \approx 25\$, about eleven times the observed
value. This reproduces the familiar picture: structure formation gives an upper
edge, and the weight is flat below it, so nothing penalizes \$\Lambda \to 0\$.
The mismatch of section 4 does not change this, because in the toy its
steady-state value is microphysical and independent of \$\Lambda\$. The ladder
multiplies the cosmological factor by a constant.

## 6. Coupling lifetime to cosmic time

To make \$T\$ depend on cosmology, the causal-diamond idea[^13][^14] is used. The
diamond of a worldline from the big bang to infinity has comoving radius
\$r(\eta)=\min(\eta,\ \eta_\infty-\eta)\$ at conformal time \$\eta\$, where
\$\eta_\infty\$ is set by the event horizon. A lineage formed at comoving
distance \$\chi\$ is active during \$[t_f+\tau,\ t_f+\tau+T]\$ and counts only
while it is inside the diamond. Summing over star formation gives

$$
W(\lambda) = \int 4\pi\chi^2\, d\chi \int dF(t_f)\;
\Big|\,[t_f+\tau,\ t_f+\tau+T]
\cap [t_{\rm in}(\chi),\ t_{\rm out}(\chi)]\,\Big| ,
$$

with \$\tau = 5\$ Gyr as a delay for observers to evolve and \$T = 10\$ Gyr as a
lifetime, both assumed. The comparison with a lineage of unbounded lifetime
(\$T=\infty\$) is the limit of rung 4.

| \$\lambda\$ | \$W\$ (T = 10 Gyr) | \$W\$ (T = \$\infty\$) | \$F(\infty)\$ alone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.001 | 31 | 4100 | 0.96 |
| 0.1 | 7.4 | 24 | 0.83 |
| 1 | 0.51 | 0.71 | 0.65 |
| 10 | 0.0036 | 0.0037 | 0.32 |
| 100 | \$1.5\times10^{-7}\$ | \$1.5\times10^{-7}\$ | 0.03 |

With a finite lifetime the weight now rises toward small \$\lambda\$ instead of
staying flat, because a larger diamond contains more matter. The median is
\$\lambda \approx 0.19\$, about a tenth of the observed value, and the observed
\$\Lambda\$ sits at about the 94th percentile. The sign of the tension has
flipped relative to section 5, where we sat at the 9th percentile.

For unbounded lifetimes the weight scales as roughly \$1/\lambda\$ at small
\$\lambda\$ (fitted slope \$-1.04\$), which is not normalizable under a flat
prior. It has the same scaling as the de Sitter entropy,
\$S_{\rm dS} \propto 1/\Lambda\$, and agrees with the earlier finding that the
observer weight is monotonic toward small \$\Lambda\$. I have not shown that
this is more than a coincidence of the crude model. If it is not, it would mean
the rung-4 lineage weight tracks the horizon entropy, and the lower edge of the
allowed range has to come from the prior, where Baum and Hawking's argument
pushes toward \$\Lambda \to 0\$[^15][^16].

Two caveats about the small-\$\lambda\$ tail. A fixed mass threshold lets
collapse keep feeding star formation indefinitely, which is unphysical. And I
extrapolated the tail mass below \$\lambda = 10^{-3}\$ using the local slope, so
the percentiles are sensitive to it. I also did not reproduce the published
causal entropic calculation[^14], so none of these numbers should be compared
quantitatively with it.

## 7. A detectability postulate *(C)*

Suppose we postulate that the relevant observers are those who can *detect*
\$\Lambda\$. This is a selection on the data and not only on existence, and it
fits the framework: for observers with physical clocks, \$\Lambda\$ is a central
label, which cannot be read off as a conjugate variable and can only be inferred
from the dynamics of \$ a(t) \$. "Can measure \$\Lambda\$" then means that the
observer's records carry non-zero mutual information with a central label.

The simplest implementation counts a lineage's time only after
\$\rho_\Lambda/\rho_m \ge \varepsilon\$, so \$\Lambda\$ has become detectable.
Everything else is as in section 6:

| \$\varepsilon\$ | median \$\lambda\$ | 16–84% range | observed sits at |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.03 | 0.22 | 0.05–1.06 | 93rd percentile |
| 0.1 | 0.31 | 0.08–1.29 | 92nd percentile |
| 0.3 | 0.61 | 0.18–2.02 | 86th percentile |
| 1.0 | 1.32 | 0.42–3.62 | 70th percentile |

A peak appears. Structure formation caps \$\Lambda\$ from above and
detectability cuts it from below, and the position of the peak tracks
\$\varepsilon\$ roughly as \$\varepsilon^{0.6}\$. The observed value falls
inside the 16–84% range only at \$\varepsilon \approx 1\$.

This does not survive unbounded lifetimes. For \$T = \infty\$ the median falls
to \$\lambda \approx 0.01\$ to \$0.03\$ for every \$\varepsilon\$ I tried, and
the observed value sits above the 96th percentile. A long-lived lineage can
simply wait until \$\Lambda\$ becomes detectable. So the mechanism works only
for observers who are short-lived compared with the time \$\Lambda\$ takes to
become detectable, which puts it in direct tension with the open-ended growth of
rung 4.

## 8. What fixes \$\varepsilon\$? An information requirement

If \$\varepsilon\$ is to be derived and not chosen, it should follow from an
information requirement. Take an observer who records the Hubble diagram of
\$N\$ standard candles at redshifts \$z \le 1\$, each with distance-modulus
error \$\sigma\$, assuming flatness is known independently and marginalizing
over the absolute magnitude. Require that the records separate "\$\Lambda\$
present" from "\$\Lambda=0\$" by at least \$\kappa\$ nats,

$$
D_{\rm KL}\big(p(\mathcal{D}\,|\,\Lambda)\,\|\,p(\mathcal{D}\,|\,0)\big)
\;\ge\; \kappa ,
$$

which for Gaussian errors is \$\Delta\chi^2/2\$. I used \$\kappa = 4.5\$,
roughly a \$3\sigma\$ detection. Solving for the smallest
\$\varepsilon = \rho_\Lambda/\rho_m\$ at the observer's epoch that meets it:

| Records | \$\varepsilon\$ required |
| --- | --- |
| \$N=10\$, \$\sigma=0.2\$ mag | 2.6 |
| \$N=50\$, \$\sigma=0.2\$ (roughly 1998) | 0.75 |
| \$N=1000\$, \$\sigma=0.15\$ | 0.10 |
| \$N=10^5\$, \$\sigma=0.15\$, no systematics | 0.009 |
| \$N \ge 10^5\$, 0.01 mag systematic floor | 0.064 |
| \$N \ge 10^8\$, 0.003 mag systematic floor | 0.019 |

At \$\kappa = 0.69\$ nats (one bit) the values drop by a factor of 2 to 3. Three
things follow.

- \$\varepsilon\$ scales as \$1/\sqrt{N}\$ until a systematics floor is reached.
  So it is set by how many records the observer has accumulated and how good its
  instruments are, not by the universe.
- There is a fundamental floor, but it is small. Plausible floors give
  \$\varepsilon \approx 0.02\$ to \$0.06\$. Plugging that into section 7, the
  median \$\lambda\$ is about 0.2 to 0.3 and the observed value sits near the
  92nd to 93rd percentile. An information-limited detectability condition pushes
  the expected \$\Lambda\$ about ten times below ours.
- The \$\varepsilon \approx 1\$ fit of section 7 corresponds to a 1998-level
  threshold. That is a statement about our technology timeline, not about what
  an observer could resolve in principle.

This is where \$T_{\rm life}\$ comes back in. The record count grows over a
lineage's lifetime, so \$\varepsilon\$ falls while \$\rho_\Lambda/\rho_m\$
rises, and the first detection happens when the two cross. For a lineage that
keeps growing the crossing always occurs eventually, even at tiny \$\Lambda\$,
which is why the \$T=\infty\$ case lost its lower edge. The cutoff survives only
for lineages that end before they reach the crossing.

## 9. What this does and does not claim

It does not claim to predict \$\Lambda\$, and it does not derive the anthropic
principle in the strong sense. The calculations show that the weight attached to
an observer lineage can be written in terms of the observer's own algebra
(mismatch accumulated over a lineage), and that this weight inherits its
\$\Lambda\$-dependence from cosmology, not from the observer. The ladder changes
the gate (who counts), not the distribution.

What I think survives:

1. **Conditioning** is plausibly not an added postulate in de Sitter space,
   because the theory has no observables without an observer *(C)*.
2. **The measure** has a natural candidate in the trace *(C)*.
3. **The reference class** can be tightened structurally, and a lineage
   requirement may exclude Boltzmann fluctuations *(C)*.
4. **The value of \$\Lambda\$** is not selected. The upper edge comes from
   structure formation, the lower edge from the prior, and a detectability
   postulate that supplies a lower edge works only for short-lived observers and
   with a threshold the framework does not fix.

The weakest steps, in the order I trust them least:

1. **The trace as typicality.** The identification of the self-sampling
   assumption with the tracial weight is unchecked.
2. **The lineage gate.** I did not compute the cost of a fluctuation that
   produces a lineage, so the Boltzmann side is open.
3. **The \$1/\lambda\$ scaling** of the unbounded-lifetime weight could be an
   artefact of the crude star-formation model, and its match with \$S_{\rm dS}\$
   is unproven.
4. **The star-formation model.** A fixed mass threshold, constant efficiency, an
   assumed normalization and assumed delay and lifetime. The small-\$\lambda\$
   tail is extrapolated.
5. **The detectability forecast.** It assumes flatness, Gaussian errors and a
   crude systematics floor, and the threshold \$\kappa\$ changes \$\varepsilon\$
   by a factor of 2 to 3.
6. **The toy observer.** The three-level Lindblad system is not a
   crossed-product algebra, and the mismatch was not computed in a gravitational
   setting.

And one worry I cannot shake: selecting on "can measure \$\Lambda\$" is
selecting on the outcome, and an observer in a tiny-\$\Lambda\$ universe would
measure an upper bound and not nothing. The postulate picks a subset, and which
subset is a choice.

## 10. What would count as evidence

| Test | What it constrains | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tracial weight on the CLPW observer algebra vs. self-sampling | trace as typicality (1) | proposed |
| Cost of a fluctuation that produces a growth lineage | lineage gate, Boltzmann side | not computed |
| Replace the collapsed-fraction proxy with a physical star-formation history | the \$\Lambda\$ tail of sections 5 to 6 | proposed |
| Derive \$\varepsilon\$ from a mutual-information bound on a clock-bearing algebra | detectability threshold | proposed |
| Model \$N(t)\$ growth and solve for the first-detection epoch | the \$T_{\rm life}\$ dependence | proposed |

The next step I would take is the last one: make the record count a function of
lineage age and solve for the epoch at which detection first becomes possible,
which makes the dependence on lifetime explicit and removes it as a free
parameter.
