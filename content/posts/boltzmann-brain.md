## Summary

The Boltzmann brain problem is usually stated as a count: in a universe that
reaches equilibrium, fluctuated brains outnumber evolved observers. This
article separates two versions of that count. The worst case is the naked
brain, a fluctuation whose memories correlate with nothing. The milder case is
a fluctuation whose observer has a true history behind it, produced by a real
entropy-increasing process.

Requiring an aligned thermodynamic arrow over the interval behind the observer
removes the worst case, and with it the argument that reasoning and records
would be unreliable. It does not remove the milder case. Fluctuations with a
local true history cost between \$10^{71}\$ and \$10^{100}\$ nats, against
\$10^{122}\$ for the observed cosmos, so they still win the count by
\$e^{10^{122}-D}\$ in an ensemble that reaches equilibrium. The arrow turns out
to be the Past Hypothesis restated as a condition on observers. The only
derived suppression that remains is a finite lifetime, as in vacuum energy
sequestering.[^1][^2]

| Question | Result |
|---|---|
| Does an aligned arrow remove naked brains? | Yes |
| Does it remove Boltzmann solar systems? | No, gap of \$10^{122}\$ remains |
| Does fabricated sky data defeat true-record requirements? | Yes, at the data level |
| Where does suppression come from? | Finite lifetime or conditioning |

## 1. Cost accounting

In de Sitter space with Hubble rate \$H\$, the equilibrium state is the
maximum-entropy state. The entropy deficit of a state holding energy
\$E = Mc^2\$ above the vacuum is approximately

$$
D(M) = \frac{Mc^2}{k_B T_{dS}}, \qquad
T_{dS} = \frac{\hbar H}{2\pi k_B}.
$$

For \$H \approx 2.2\times 10^{-18}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\$ this gives
\$T_{dS} \approx 2.7\times 10^{-30}\$ K. A macrostate filling the Hubble
volume (about \$10^{53}\$ kg) costs \$D_{cos} \approx 10^{122.4}\$ nats. The
weight of a fluctuation of cost \$D\$ relative to the observed cosmos is
\$e^{D_{cos}-D}\$, so any class with \$D \ll D_{cos}\$ dominates the count
in an equilibrium ensemble.[^3][^4]

The accounting is crude. The Hubble-volume matter exceeds the Nariai bound,
so the cosmological row is indicative only.

## 2. The entropy profile of a fluctuation

Under detailed balance the most probable path into a fluctuation is the time
reverse of the relaxation out of it. The entropy profile is therefore
time-symmetric about its minimum: entropy rises toward both the past and the
future of the minimum.

An observer sitting at the minimum has memories that correlate with states of
higher entropy than its own. Those records are produced by no earlier process.
This is the naked Boltzmann brain, and its memories are correlations in the
wrong thermodynamic direction.

An observer on the slope is different. Records form in the direction of rising
entropy, so an observer at time \$\tau\$ after the minimum has an aligned arrow
over the interval \$\tau\$ behind it, and its records were generated by the
dynamics. The reach of its records is the length of the interval behind the
observer over which entropy increases monotonically.

This replaces the earlier definition of reach through the truth of
referents, which depended on a semantic assumption (Assumption A of the
previous article).

## 3. Cost of the aligned class

The minimum state, at time \$-\tau\$, must be able to evolve into the
observer with its records. The cheapest such state holds the matter and free
energy that the history needs:

| Aligned history behind the observer | \$\log_{10} D\$ | Gap \$\log_{10}(D_{cos}-D)\$ |
|---|---|---|
| None (naked brain) | 69.5 | 122.4 |
| A human life (body and 80 yr at 100 W) | 71.2 | 122.4 |
| Gyr biosphere (Earth mass as floor) | 94.2 | 122.4 |
| Sun and Earth, 4.5 Gyr | 99.7 | 122.4 |
| Galaxy | 111.7 | 122.4 |
| Hubble ball (the observed history) | 122.4 | 0 |

Two points follow from the table. Over a human lifetime the arrow adds about
\$10^{64}\$ nats of metabolic budget on top of the body at \$10^{71.2}\$, which
is negligible. Over a Gyr history the cost rises to about \$10^{94}\$, a large
number that is still smaller than \$D_{cos}\$ by a factor of \$10^{28}\$ in the
exponent.

## 4. What the criterion removes

The naked brain has no reliable records. A reasoner who takes itself to be a
typical member of that class has no grounds for trusting any inference,
including the inference to the class. This cognitive instability is the
sharpest objection to equilibrium cosmologies, and the aligned-arrow
criterion removes the class that carries it.[^5]

The criterion also gives the epistemic test in structural form. A fluctuation
predicts disordered future data, whereas an observer with an aligned arrow
predicts continued order. The observed order is therefore evidence for the
aligned class over the naked class, though not over a Boltzmann solar system.

## 5. What it does not remove

Every aligned fluctuation with local history costs far less than a
Hubble-scale macrostate. In an ensemble that reaches equilibrium the expected
number of such observers per Hubble time and volume is

$$
N_{\mathrm{BB}} \approx \frac{T}{t_H}\, e^{-D},
$$

and the comparison with the observed cosmos is \$e^{D_{cos}-D}\$. The problem
changes from Boltzmann brains to Boltzmann solar systems and is not
resolved.

## 6. Fabricated sky data

The observed records include the CMB and distant galaxies. The cheapest
fluctuation whose referents truly exist at that scale is the cosmos itself,
which would restore \$D = D_{cos}\$. But the requirement that referents
exist is not a requirement on the data.

A fluctuation can include a thin beam of incoming photons shaped as an image
of the sky. The beam needs to fill only a telescope aperture, so its energy is
tiny and its information content is of order \$10^{20}\$ nats at most. Both are
negligible against the body at \$10^{71}\$. The arrow stays aligned, the local
records stay true (they correlate with the beam), and the sky data are
indistinguishable from the real thing.

Only the semantic requirement, that records correlate with real galaxies
rather than with the photons that arrived, forces the cost up to
\$D_{cos}\$. That is a condition on the truth of the observer's world-model.
It is a postulate and cannot be derived from the records.

## 7. The arrow is the Past Hypothesis

At cosmic reach, an aligned arrow requires a low-entropy initial macrostate.
In the tracial measure that state costs \$e^{-10^{122}}\$. The Boltzmann
problem and the Past Hypothesis problem are therefore the same problem seen
from two sides.[^6] The arrow criterion does not supply a suppression of its
own. It states what must be assumed, or derived, for the equilibrium epoch to
be irrelevant.

## 8. Where suppression can come from

Suppression is available only from the structure of the theory.

**Finite lifetime.** A universe that lasts less than \$e^{D}\$ Hubble times
contains negligible fluctuated observers of cost \$D\$. For a brain,
fluctuations matter only if \$\ln(T/t_H)\$ exceeds a few times \$10^{69}\$.
Vacuum energy sequestering requires finite four-volume and has no empty de
Sitter solution: for pure vacuum energy the right-hand side of the field
equation vanishes identically, so the Euclidean empty sphere is not a
saddle.[^2] With a lifetime of about 10 Hubble times the expected number of
fluctuated brains is about \$10\, e^{-4\times 10^{69}}\$, and aligned
solar-system fluctuations at \$10^{94}\$ to \$10^{100}\$ are suppressed far
more. This is the only derived suppression found in the program, and it is
conditional on sequestering.

**Henneaux–Teitelboim fixed volume.** Empty de Sitter is a solution, with
\$Z(V) \approx e^{2S_{dS}(V)}\$, so the fluctuation-dominance obstruction is
not avoided.[^7]

**Conditioning on the initial macrostate.** This works by postulate, as in
Hartle–Hertog-style proposals, and does not remove the equilibrium epoch from
the theory.

## 9. Status and open problems

1. Whether a finite-volume theory with a quantum-gravity completion retains a
   dominant de Sitter sphere saddle. If it does, the finite-lifetime route
   fails.
2. A derivation, inside the algebra, of why true records at scale
   \$\ell\$ require a macrostate of scale \$\ell\$. The identity
   \$D = I(R;E) + D(\rho_R) + D(\rho_E)\$ gives only a bound of \$I\$ nats,
   about \$10^{15}\$ for a human memory, which is far too weak.
3. Splitting rung 1 of the observer ladder into a private clock and a shared
   background clock. An equilibrium algebra has no background clock, which
   favors the shared-clock algebras, but does not derive that choice.[^8]
4. The off-shell Euclidean path integral with \$\Lambda\$ and \$\lambda\$
   integrated, which none of the checks above examines.
